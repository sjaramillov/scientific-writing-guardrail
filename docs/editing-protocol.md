# Editing a passage for a human reader

Use this protocol in Codex, in another editing environment, or with a human editor. A first pass fits one argument. Define the reader by the task they need to perform, for example a field mycologist deciding whether a technical result supports a biological interpretation.

1. **Freeze the source.** Record its title, version, locator and evidence reference. Keep the original, figures and underlying evidence available privately. Identify whether the input is a manuscript, AI draft, software result, formal proof or synopsis. Trace the draft to the experiment, proof, or paper on which it relies. Flag any missing link.
2. **Build the claim map.** Give each assertion a stable ID, an exact source passage and evidence status. Record subject, population, method, comparison, quantity and units, uncertainty, time, author, references and limitations when applicable. Distinguish what a source reports from what the authors independently checked. Record contradictions and unresolved citations as questions.
3. **Write a coherent paragraph.** Start with the reader's question. Introduce the object before a pronoun or acronym. Carry the same specimen, dataset or mathematical object across sentences. Place conditions next to results and explain why the result answers the question. Expand a key term on first use. Prefer a precise technical term when a familiar synonym would change meaning. Check grammar, agreement, tense, parallel clauses and transitions aloud. Keep technical detail in a companion note when it would interrupt the argument; give the reader the reference needed to recover it.
4. **Translate claim by claim and then read the whole passage.** Use idiomatic EN and ES word order. Pair each rendering with the same claim ID. Maintain units, significant figures, symbols, negations, comparison group, causal strength, citation and uncertainty. Examine decimal separators, false friends and grammatical gender where they could change the referent. A human competent in both languages judges whether the terms fit the discipline.
5. **Check the packet.** Each protected idea has a verbatim source expression and an expected expression in each language. Run the CLI against the frozen file. Inspect changed emphasis, claims omitted from the map, added assertions, causal verbs, modifiers and misplaced citations manually. A literal match cannot detect all these changes. Confirm each reference exists and that its specific passage supports the claim.
6. **Elicit understanding.** Give the passage to a representative reader and ask a content question and a boundary question before revealing the answer key. A second reviewer checks technical fidelity. Keep their actual words and decisions private. Record role, date, source and packet hashes, disagreements and required revisions. Recheck after changes. Declare editorial assistance according to the chosen journal's current policy.

## Reusable editorial instruction

> Edit the supplied passage for the stated reader. First reconstruct what the evidence actually establishes, including who performed the work and what was excluded. Return the revised technical paragraph in English, a natural Spanish rendering, a claim-to-source map, and unresolved questions for the author. Make the reasoning followable and keep each qualification beside its claim. Use the source for every factual assertion. Where a step is missing, state what evidence or explanation would connect it. Leave scientific sign-off and observed reader comprehension pending until real reviewers provide them.

## Human review record

Record the packet hash, audience, source locator and date. For each claim, record **supported**, **needs revision**, or **unresolved**, and explain the decision. Review the finished paragraph for continuity, grammatical clarity and technical terminology in both languages. Store the reader's answers separately from the expected answers. Only a completed human review can supply a reviewer identity or decision.
